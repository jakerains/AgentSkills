# Worked example: one real interaction

This is a fictional planning example, not evidence that a particular product has these features. Replace every asset and factual behavior with inspected project material before using the plan in production.

## Brief

Audience: current product users who repeat the same filtering task.

Takeaway: "A saved view returns me to the same filtered results."

Inputs: authorized logo, actual screen captures for initial results, applied filters, save action, and reopened saved view. Quiet voiceover explaining those exact actions. Wide desktop delivery only, approximately 16 seconds after measuring narration.

Direction: editorial close-up of the real interface. Use the existing product type and colors. Keep the results panel as a spatial anchor. Use one restrained expansion into the proof, sparse contact sounds, and stable reading holds.

## Replace activity with a story

Weak plan: headline fades in, cards bounce, dashboard zooms, particles appear, logo fades in.

Useful plan:

| Time | Viewer learns | Entry -> exit state | Attention and continuity | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0-2s | Repeating the setup is the friction | Actual initial results -> filter area emphasized | Establish the real results panel and a single relevant control | Voice establishes the task; quiet bed |
| 2-5s | These filters produce the desired set | Initial results -> real filtered results | Cursor action leads; panel stays anchored; results settle before emphasis | Click at contact, then space for the explanation |
| 5-9s | This exact view can be saved | Filtered results -> actual saved state | Maintain the same panel; show the supported save interaction | Small confirmation cue at the visible result |
| 9-13s | Reopening preserves the setup | Real navigation state -> reopened view | Match the filter labels and actual displayed results; hold the proof | Voice names what persisted; reduce the music here |
| 13-16s | The repeatable action is clear | Settled proof -> readable closing instruction | Use the real action label; design the closing frame as a cover | Resolve the phrase and allow its tail |

Resolve the provisional seconds to actual frame boundaries after measuring voice and selecting FPS. Do not time-compress the demonstration into an unsupported speed claim. If a supplied recording lacks the reopened state, stop that proof shot and obtain it; a fabricated screenshot cannot complete the example.

## Small details worth specifying

- Keep the filter labels visible during the proof rather than replacing them with a generic checkmark.
- Give the cursor a destination and visible contact; let the response follow that contact.
- Hold the result before the next headline competes for attention.
- Zoom toward the relevant region while retaining an anchor that explains where it is in the full UI.
- Choose a confirmation sound with a clear onset; compensate for silence inside the source file.
- Put captions outside the working region and avoid narrating four labels while also showing a new headline.
- Capture the opening, filtered state, save contact, transition midpoint, reopened proof, cover, and final frame.

## Example critique

Observed at 9.2s: the incoming headline overlaps the filter labels while the camera is still moving. The viewer cannot compare the before/after configuration.

Local repair: complete the camera move first, hold the actual filter labels, and move or delay the headline. Recheck that interval with sound and the next handoff. Do not redesign the full film because one proof beat failed.
